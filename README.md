## Hi, I'm Abdullah

Most people call me Hazeq. I'm 21, I live in the UAE, and I run a small technology company called ZEQLAR while I look for my next role on a team.

I started ZEQLAR because businesses around me needed software and I knew how to build it. Running it has taught me more than anything else I've done. I learned to listen properly before writing any code, to design interfaces people enjoy using, and to stand behind the work after it ships. I design every screen myself now, and every client gets that same care, whether I'm building them a product, a website or the content that goes on it.

I also know how much I still have to learn. The quickest way I know to grow is to work beside people who are better than me, so I'm looking for a full-time role in AI engineering, full stack development, or anywhere a client meets the technology. ZEQLAR keeps running alongside, and everything I learn on a team makes it better too.

I won't pretend to be the most experienced person you'll interview. What I can promise is that I'll learn your stack quickly, adapt to whatever the work needs, and work as hard as anyone you've ever hired. Ask anyone I've worked with.

## The name

Hazeq (حاذق) is my family name. In Arabic it means skilled, adept, masterful and sharp, all at once. It's what I ask people to call me, and it's the standard I'm trying to grow into.

Right now I'm a jack of all trades. I build the front end and the back end, design the interface, train the model, scope the project and look after the client. I see that as a starting point. Every master of all trades began as a jack of all trades, and I intend to get there one skill at a time.

## Why I do this

I want to do my best work, earn well from it, and use it to look after the people who need it most.

That starts with my own clients. The market is hard for a lot of small businesses right now, and good software shouldn't only belong to companies with big budgets. If a client is going through a difficult stretch, we work out instalments, or start with a smaller first version they can afford and grow it from there. I'd rather build something that helps than turn someone away.
